## 1. Introduction and scope

### 1.1 Subject

GasNetSim 4Qgis is a QGIS plugin that solves the steady-state hydraulic
problem of a natural gas transmission network, optionally blended with
hydrogen, starting from the vector representation of the network held in a
QGIS project.

The plugin acts as the geospatial interface to the **GasNetSim** solver
(Forschungszentrum Jülich, ICE-1) and extends it with compressor station
handling, parallel execution of hourly scenarios, and cartographic rendering of
the results.

### 1.2 Model solved

The model is **nodal**, **steady-state** and **isothermal**, with a real-gas
equation of state. The unknowns are the nodal pressures; flow rates, velocities
and thermophysical properties are derived quantities.

The complete formulation, with the equations as implemented, is given in §2. In
summary the model comprises:

- a mass balance at each demand node;
- the general flow equation for each pipeline, with the friction factor from
  the explicit correlation of Chen (1979);
- a geodetic elevation correction term;
- the **GERG-2008** equation of state (ISO 20765-2) for compressibility factor,
  density and heat capacities;
- composition tracking where gases of different composition are injected.

### 1.3 Range of validity and limitations

| Aspect | Scope |
|--------|-------|
| Regime | Steady state. Transients are not modelled: each run describes the asymptotic state of the network at constant demand. |
| Temperature | Uniform and prescribed. The thermal balance along pipelines is not solved. |
| Phase | Single-phase gas. Condensates and two-phase transport are not treated. |
| Pressures | Verified on 1st and 2nd class networks (24–75 barg). The formulation imposes no explicit pressure limits. |
| Components | Pipelines, demand and injection nodes, fixed-pressure nodes, compressor stations. Control valves, regulators and storage are not exposed by the interface. |
| Optimisation | Not provided. The plugin solves the configuration supplied; it does not search for an optimal one. |

Beyond the topological checks of §4, the plugin performs no plausibility
checking of the input data: missing values are replaced by the fallback values
documented in §6.2 and the computation proceeds. Ensuring the physical
consistency of the data remains the user's responsibility.

### 1.4 Typical applications

1. **Adequacy assessment**: determination of pressures at delivery points for a
   given hourly demand profile, and comparison against contractual minima.
2. **Hydrogen blending analysis**: assessment of the effect of a molar fraction
   of H₂ on pressures, velocities, heating value, specific gravity and Wobbe
   index, for verification of gas quality constraints (§13.4).
3. **Reinforcement sizing**: comparison of alternative network configurations
   at equal demand.
4. **Multi-scenario campaigns**: automatic execution of 24 hourly scenarios, or
   of a series of days, with systematic collection of results.

### 1.5 Processing architecture

The workflow is organised in three stages, matching the three tabs of the
interface:

| Stage | Tab | Content |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Topological verification | Tab 0 | Connectivity analysis of the pipeline layer, detection of degenerate geometries, duplicates and disconnected components; correction by endpoint snapping (§4). |
| Case definition and execution | Tab 1 | Association of layer fields with model quantities, assignment of physical and numerical parameters, generation of the input files and execution of the solver (§5, §6, §7). |
| Post-processing | Tab 2 | Thematic rendering of results and export (§8). |

The order 0 → 1 → 2 is operational rather than indicative: omitting the
preliminary topological verification is the most frequent cause of
non-convergence on vector data not prepared for computation (§12.6).

### 1.6 Structure of this document

§2–§3 describe the model and the organisation of the interface; §4–§8 document
every exposed control, tab by tab; §9–§10 define the quantities returned; §11
presents a **reproducible verification case**, with the expected numerical

Quantities are denoted by the symbols and units adopted in §14. Equations are
numbered by section and referenced in the text.

## 2. Model formulation

This section reports the equations actually implemented in the solver, in the
form in which they are coded, and defines the physical meaning of the
parameters exposed by the interface. The complete notation is given in §14.

### 2.1 Network representation

The network is represented as a directed graph G = (N, P), where N is the set
of nodes and P the set of pipelines. Each pipeline p ∈ P connects an inlet node
i and an outlet node j; the orientation is a convention assigned when the model
is built and carries no physical meaning: the actual flow direction is a result
of the computation (§10.2).

Nodes are of two kinds:

- **reference nodes** (node_type = `reference`): fixed-pressure nodes, which
  constitute the boundary conditions of the problem and at which the
  composition of the injected gas is assigned;
- **demand or junction nodes**: nodes at which a volumetric flow rate at
  standard conditions is prescribed, positive if withdrawn, negative if
  injected.

### 2.2 Standard conditions and pressure conventions

Volumetric flow rates are referred to standard conditions

    T_b = 288.15 K        p_b = 1 atm = 101 325 Pa                        (2.1)

consistent with UNI EN ISO 13443. The corresponding unit is the standard cubic
metre (Sm³, equivalently sm³).

**Pressure convention.** The value assigned to reference nodes through the
*Reference pressure* parameter is interpreted as **gauge** pressure; conversion
to absolute pressure takes place when the model is built:

    p_abs = p_gauge + p_atm,     p_atm = 101 325 Pa                       (2.2)

All pressures returned by the computation (§9) are **absolute**. An assigned
value of 75 bar therefore corresponds to 76.013 bar in the results.

### 2.3 Nodal continuity equation

For every non-reference node i, conservation of mass requires

    Σ_(p ∈ P_i) s_(i,p) · Q_p − D_i = 0                                   (2.3)

where Q_p is the volumetric flow rate of pipeline p at standard conditions,
s_(i,p) = +1 if the pipeline enters the node and −1 if it leaves it, and D_i is
the nodal demand (positive for withdrawal). System (2.3), written for all
non-reference nodes, constitutes the non-linear system solved; the unknowns are
the nodal pressures.

### 2.4 Pipeline flow equation

Flow is computed with the general gas flow equation:

    Q = sgn(Δ) · C_p · √( |p₁² − p₂² − H| / (Z · SG) )                    (2.4)

with Δ = p₁² − p₂² − H. The term C_p collects the physical characteristics of
the pipeline:

    C_p = K · (T_b / p_b) · D^2.5 · √( 1 / (L · T_avg · f) ) · η          (2.5)

    K = π · √( R / (16 · M_air) ),    M_air = 28.97 g/mol                 (2.6)

where D is the internal diameter, L the length, T_avg the average temperature,
f the friction factor, η the pipeline efficiency factor and R the universal gas
constant.

The average pressure in the pipeline, used to evaluate gas properties, is
computed in the form customary for gas flow:

    p_avg = (2/3) · [ (p₁ + p₂) − (p₁ · p₂)/(p₁ + p₂) ]                   (2.7)

which, because of compressibility, differs from the arithmetic mean.

#### 2.4.1 Elevation correction

The term H accounts for the difference in elevation between the two ends:

    H = 0.06835 · SG · (h₂ − h₁) · p_avg² / (Z · T_avg)                   (2.8)

with h₁, h₂ the elevations of the inlet and outlet nodes. The term vanishes for
horizontal pipelines. For gas at 70 bar the density is of the order of
70 kg/m³: over elevation differences of a few hundred metres the contribution
of (2.8) is comparable with that of friction, and in mountainous networks it
may dominate. It follows that the accuracy of nodal elevations matters as much
as that of diameters (§5.4, §6.2).

#### 2.4.2 Friction factor

The friction factor is computed with the explicit correlation of **Chen
(1979)**, valid over the whole turbulent range and in the transition:

    1/√f = −2 · log₁₀[ ε/(3.7065·D)
             − (5.0452/Re) · log₁₀( (ε/D)^1.1098 / 2.8257
             + (7.149/Re)^0.8981 ) ]                                      (2.9)

where ε is the absolute wall roughness (*Pipe roughness* parameter, §6.2) and
Re the Reynolds number, evaluated with the dynamic viscosity of the mixture
computed by the Lucas and Herning-Zipperer methods.

The choice of correlation is fixed by the plugin (`friction_method = chen` in
the input files) and cannot be changed from the interface.

### 2.5 Equation of state and gas properties

The compressibility factor Z, density, standard density, molar mass and heat
capacities of the mixture are computed with the **GERG-2008** equation of state
(ISO 20765-2), as functions of pressure, temperature and molar composition, and
are updated node by node at every iteration.

For an ideal gas Z = 1; under conditions typical of transmission networks
(70–76 bar, 288 K) Z ≈ 0.80–0.88 (§9.3). Neglecting this departure would
introduce errors of the order of 15–20 % in density and, consequently, in the
computed velocities.

Specific gravity is defined as

    SG = M_gas / M_air                                                    (2.10)

and the Wobbe index as

    W = HHV / √SG                                                         (2.11)

with HHV the higher heating value referred to standard conditions.

### 2.6 Composition tracking

Where gases of different composition are injected, the composition at each node
is determined by **simple mixing** of the incoming flows weighted by their
respective compositions. Since composition modifies the properties appearing in
(2.4), the hydraulic and composition problems are coupled and are solved with a
**staggered** scheme: at each outer iteration the pressure field is solved with
frozen composition, the composition is then updated, and the process is
repeated until both variations fall below their respective thresholds.

The staggered scheme is enabled automatically when at least one non-reference
node carries its own composition (§5.7); otherwise the solver adopts a direct
scheme. The difference between the two schemes is not merely formal: it affects
the robustness of convergence (§12.3).

### 2.7 Numerical solution

System (2.3), non-linear because of the dependence of Q on pressures expressed
by (2.4), is solved with the **Newton-Raphson** method. Denoting by F(x) the
vector of nodal balance residuals and by J(x) = ∂F/∂x the Jacobian matrix, at
each iteration the linear system

    J(x⁽ᵏ⁾) · Δx = F(x⁽ᵏ⁾)                                               (2.12)

is solved and the state is updated as

    x⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾ = x⁽ᵏ⁾ + Δx / URF                                             (2.13)

**The URF appears in the denominator.** A large value reduces the step length
and therefore *damps* the iteration, increasing stability at the expense of
speed; a value below unity amplifies the Newton step. The value set by the user
acts as a lower bound (the most aggressive step allowed): the solver raises it
automatically, up to a maximum of max(4·URF, 8), when the residual fails to
decrease satisfactorily, and returns it towards the assigned value when
convergence proceeds regularly.

At each iteration the step length is further limited by a *line search*
(successive halving until the residual decreases) and by a trust-region
constraint on the components of Δx.

#### 2.7.1 Convergence criterion

Convergence is declared when

    max |F(x)| ≤ tol                                                      (2.14)

that is, when the largest flow imbalance among all nodes falls below the
prescribed tolerance. **The tolerance is therefore a flow rate**, expressed in
Sm³/s: the default value of 0.001 Sm³/s corresponds to a maximum admissible
imbalance of 3.6 Sm³/h per node. In the verification case of §11 the residual
imbalance is precisely of this order (§11.6), which explains why the returned
flow rates do not close the balance to arbitrary precision.

If (2.14) is not satisfied within the prescribed maximum number of iterations,
the solver returns the last computed iterate, flagged as a **non-converged
solution**: an intermediate state that does not satisfy the nodal balances and
has no physical meaning (§7.5, §12).

## 3. Interface architecture

### 3.1 Launching

The plugin is launched from the QGIS toolbar icon or from **Plugins →
GasNetSim_4Qgis**. A single resizable window opens, independent of the main
QGIS window: the map remains accessible during configuration and computation.

### 3.2 Window organisation

The window consists of a collapsible side bar and a three-tab container.

#### 3.2.1 Side bar

Opened and closed with the **☰** button at the top left.

| Command | Function |
|---------|----------|
| ⚙ Settings | Selection of the interface language (Italian/English). The change takes effect the next time the plugin is opened. |
| Guide | Opens the present document. |
| ℹ About | Version, author, repository, licence. |

#### 3.2.2 Tabs

| Tab | Designation | Reference |
|-----|-------------|-----------|
| Tab 0 | Check Pipelines | §4 |
| Tab 1 | Prepare & Simulate | §5, §6, §7 |
| Tab 2 | View Results | §8 |

### 3.3 Structure of Tab 1

Tab 1 is divided into two columns separated by a movable splitter.

**Left column** — scrollable configuration form, articulated in six blocks:

| Block | Title | Visibility | § |
|-------|-------|------------|---|
| 1 | Input Data | always visible | 5.2 |
| 2 | Collapse Options / Already Collapsed Layers and field mapping | depends on mode | 5.3–5.5 |
| 3 | ⚙ Compressors | on request | 6.1 |
| 4 | ⚙ Advanced options | on request | 6.2 |
| 5 | ⚙ Solver parameters | on request | 6.3 |
| 6 | Gas Composition | always visible | 6.4 |

Blocks marked ⚙ are collapsed on opening and expand when the corresponding
checkbox is ticked. This arrangement keeps visible the minimum set of
parameters required for a simulation, deferring the physical and numerical
parameters to those who need to modify them.

**Right column** — execution commands (§7.1) and the log window. The log
reports in real time the stages of the computation, the parameters actually
adopted and the outcome; it is the primary reference for diagnostics (§12). The
log's context menu allows it to be cleared.

### 3.4 Treatment of user data

The plugin does not modify the source layers: the topological layers, result
layers, CSV files and GeoPackages produced are all new items (§7.6).

The single exception is explicit and subject to confirmation: in Tab 0, the
endpoint snapping operation may be applied to the original layer instead of a
copy (§4.3); if the layer is file-based, the change is irreversible.

### 3.5 Coordinate reference system

Internal geometric processing (graph construction, node snapping, length
computation) is carried out in **EPSG:25833** (ETRS89 / UTM zone 33N). Layers
in other reference systems are reprojected.

Consequently the metric quantities exposed by the interface — *Snap radius*
(§6.2) and the snapping *Tolerance* (§4.3) — are expressed in metres. Using
layers in geographic coordinates (EPSG:4326) is discouraged: tolerances would
have to be expressed in degrees and computed lengths would be meaningless.

### 3.6 Elements not present

To avoid fruitless searching, the interface does **not** provide:

- a dedicated tab for simulation parameters: these reside in blocks 4–6 of
  Tab 1;
- a field for the folder of input CSV files: the folder is unique and is
  specified in block 1 of Tab 1 (§5.2);
- a selection of friction correlation or equation of state: these are fixed to
  Chen (1979) and GERG-2008 respectively (§2.4.2, §2.5).

## 4. Tab 0 — Topological verification of the network

### 4.1 Purpose

Topological verification precedes the construction of the computational model.
System (2.3) admits a unique solution only if every connected component of the
network contains at least one fixed-pressure node: a disconnected portion
without a reference node makes the system indeterminate and the simulation
fails to converge (§12.6).

Connectivity is a **topological, not a graphical** property: two pipelines
whose endpoints lie a few centimetres apart appear contiguous at the display
scale but constitute two distinct components. This tab detects this and other
pathologies typical of vector data not originally produced for modelling.

### 4.2 Network analysis

#### 4.2.1 Layer selection

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Pipelines** | Line layer to be analysed. Required. |
| **Nodes (opt.)** | Optional point layer. If assigned, the snapping operation (§4.3) attaches pipeline endpoints to existing nodes within tolerance, taking priority over the cluster centroid. To be used when node positions are surveyed and considered reliable. |

#### 4.2.2 Procedure

The **Analyze network** command performs, read-only:

1. repair of invalid geometries;
2. **explosion of polylines** into elementary segments, which constitute the
   graph edges. The resulting number of edges is generally larger than the
   number of layer features;
3. construction of the graph and counting of edges discarded as null or
   degenerate;
4. identification of pathologies or, in their absence, the message
   `[OK] Nessun problema topologico rilevato.`

#### 4.2.3 Pathologies detected

| Pathology | Definition | Consequence for the computation |
|-----------|------------|----------------------------------|
| Null or degenerate geometries | Features without geometry or of zero length | Pipelines with L = 0: equation (2.5) diverges |
| Duplicates and overlaps | Edges with the same pair of endpoints, or overlapping | Non-physical duplication of the transport capacity of that stretch |
| Disconnected components (islands) | Subgraphs not connected to the main graph | Indeterminate system in the absence of a reference node in the component |

The option **Automatically create a coloured layer if islands are found**
(enabled by default) generates a temporary layer with a distinct colour per
component, allowing discontinuities to be located immediately.

The **⚠ Select problem pipelines** command selects, in the source layer, the
duplicate or overlapping edges identified by the last analysis.

### 4.3 Endpoint snapping

A geometric correction operation that unifies mutually close endpoints. It is
the only operation of the plugin capable of modifying the source data.

| Parameter | Default | Description |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Tolerance** | 1.0 m | Maximum distance within which two endpoints are considered coincident. Expressed in the layer's CRS units (cf. §3.5). |
| **Strategy — Midpoint** | selected | All endpoints of the cluster are moved to the cluster centroid. A neutral choice, appropriate when sources have homogeneous accuracy. |
| **Strategy — Anchor to first** | — | One endpoint remains fixed and the others are moved onto it. Appropriate when one stretch has superior planimetric accuracy. |
| **Output — Create new layer** | selected | Produces a corrected copy, added to the project. The original is left unchanged. |
| **Output — Modify original layer** | — | Writes into the source layer. For file-based layers the change is **irreversible**; explicit confirmation is required. |

If a node layer has been assigned (§4.2.1), endpoints are snapped to existing
nodes within tolerance.

#### 4.3.1 Criterion for choosing the tolerance

The tolerance must exceed the positional error of the data and remain below the
minimum distance between genuinely distinct elements. An excessive value merges
parallel pipelines belonging to the same corridor, producing a network that
does not exist and, typically, overestimated transport capacity.

An incremental procedure is recommended: start from 1–2 m, analyse, check the
reduction in the number of components, and increase the tolerance only if
necessary, inspecting the result visually at each step.

### 4.4 Islands and re-analysis

This block becomes active when disconnected components are present.

| Command | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **⚠ Select islands** | Selects, in the source layer, the edges belonging to components detached from the main graph. |
| **Create coloured layer** | Temporary layer with one colour per component. |
| **Re-analyze current network** | Repeats the analysis on the layer produced by the last snapping operation, if any, otherwise on the selected layer. |

### 4.5 Recommended procedure

1. Analysis of the source layer.
2. Where islands are present, inspection of the coloured layer.
3. Snapping with a conservative tolerance, output to a **new layer**.
4. Re-analysis of the layer produced.
5. Iteration of steps 3–4 until the remaining components correspond to portions
   of the real network that are genuinely isolated (for example branches not in
   service).
6. Removal or exclusion of residual components without supply, since they are
   incompatible with solvability of the system.
7. Proceeding to Tab 1 using the corrected layer.

> Model construction (§5.3) may itself generate warnings: these are presented
> in a dedicated window, with the **Zoom to pipelines** and **Flash**
> commands for cartographic location of the elements concerned.

## 5. Tab 1 — Input data and field mapping

### 5.1 Principle

This tab builds, from the vector layers of the QGIS project, the two files that
constitute the solver input: `sf_nodes.csv` and `sf_pipelines.csv` (§5.8). The
files are regenerated in full at every run; there are no pre-existing input
files to select.

The required information must already be present in the layers, in fields of
any name. Mapping consists in declaring the correspondence between layer fields
and model quantities. The plugin does not infer missing quantities: in their
absence it applies the fallback values of §6.2 and proceeds.

### 5.2 Block 1 — Input Data

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Pipeline layer** | Line layer. If the verification of §4 has been performed, use the corrected layer and not the original. |
| **Node layer** | Point layer. Formally optional, substantially necessary: reference nodes and nodal demands derive from it. Without it the model has no boundary conditions. |
| **DEM (raster)** | Digital elevation model, optional. Used to assign nodal elevations according to the rules of §5.4. |
| **Output folder** | Destination of input files, results and persisted layers (§7.6). Single for the whole computation. Required for the *Generate CSV* command and for parallel execution. |
| **Hour of day** | Hourly index 0–23 selecting the value to be extracted from the demand profile (§5.6). The label below reports the value read from the first node carrying a profile: it is the immediate check of correct interpretation of the field. |
| **Simulate 24 hours in parallel (0-23)** | Simultaneous execution of the 24 hourly scenarios (§7.3). |

### 5.3 Block 2 — Model construction mode

The **Network already collapsed** checkbox determines whether the computational
graph is to be built from the geometry (Mode A) or taken from the layers
(Mode B).

#### 5.3.1 Mode A — graph built from the geometry

Active when the checkbox is cleared (default condition). The plugin performs
the **topological collapse**: it explodes polylines, identifies coincident
endpoints, generates the nodes, assigns identifiers and computes lengths from
the three-dimensional geometry.

Two layers, named `Condotte #N` and `Nodi #N`, are then added to the project;
they constitute the network actually simulated.

**Behaviour to be known before adopting this mode:**

1. **Node identifiers are generated by the plugin**, by means of a progressive
   counter starting from **0** in the order endpoints are encountered while
   scanning the pipelines. Any identifiers present in the node layer are **not
   preserved**: they survive only in the `fid_ut` and `label_ut` fields of the
   `Nodi #N` layer. The correspondence between one's own nodes and those of the
   results must be reconstructed through those fields or geometrically.
2. **Pipeline identifiers** are renumbered progressively from 1.
3. **Elevations do not come from the node layer** (§5.4).

The **Collapse Options** box exposes:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Diameter** and **Unit** | Pipeline-layer field holding the internal diameter and its unit: `inches (in)` — first entry, hence the default —, `metres (m)`, `centimetres (cm)`. Automatic field recognition proceeds in the order: `diameter_m`, `diam_m`, `D`, `d`, `diam`, `Diametro_pollici`, `Diametro`, `diameter`. Checking the unit is essential: by (2.5) the flow rate depends on D^2.5, so a unit error of factor 39.37 produces a discrepancy of about ten orders of magnitude in transport capacity. |
| **Reference field** and value | Node-layer field and value identifying fixed-pressure nodes (for example `type` = `reference`, or `is_ref` = `1`). Candidates tried automatically: `matched`, `type`, `Type`, `NodeID`, `is_ref`, `reference`, `nodo_tipo`. Comparison is performed both textually and numerically. **If the value is left empty, all snapped nodes are treated as reference nodes**: the network then has pressure imposed everywhere and the results are meaningless (§12.5). |

In Mode A the hourly demand field and the snap radius reside in the
*⚙ Advanced options* block (§6.2).

#### 5.3.2 Mode B — graph taken from the layers

Active when the checkbox is ticked. It assumes that each pipeline explicitly
declares the two nodes it connects. This is the mode to be used when
identifiers and elevations must be preserved, and the one adopted in the
verification case of §11.

The layers typically used are the `Condotte #N` and `Nodi #N` produced by a
previous collapse, which the plugin preselects automatically, or layers
prepared by the user with the required fields.

### 5.4 Origin of nodal elevations

The elevation h appearing in (2.8) is determined as follows.

| Mode | Origin of the elevation |
|------|--------------------------|
| A, three-dimensional pipeline geometry (LineString Z) | Z coordinate of the vertices |
| A, two-dimensional geometry, DEM assigned | Sampling of the DEM at the node coordinates |
| A, two-dimensional geometry, no DEM | **h = 0 for all nodes**: term (2.8) vanishes |
| A, *Preserve intermediate nodes* enabled | DEM sampling for every node, intermediate ones included (§6.2) |
| B | Field mapped to *Elevation* (§5.5.2) |

The node layer's elevation field **is not used in Mode A**. This deserves
attention: a network with significant elevation differences simulated in Mode A
without a DEM and with two-dimensional geometry returns pressures lacking the
geodetic contribution, which at transmission pressures is of the order of
0.1 bar per 100 m of elevation difference.

### 5.5 Field mapping (Mode B)

#### 5.5.1 Pipelines

| Interface field | Column produced | Automatic recognition |
|-----------------|------------------|------------------------|
| **Pipe ID** | `pipeline_index` | `pipe_id`, `id`, `FID`, `fid`, `OBJECTID` |
| **Node A / B** | `inlet_index` / `outlet_index` | A: `node_a`, `from_node`, `inlet`, `a`, `start_node`; B: `node_b`, `to_node`, `outlet`, `b`, `end_node` |
| **Length** | `length_m` | `length_m`, `length`, `L`, `len`, `lunghezza`, `lung_m` |
| **Diameter** and unit | `diameter_m` | as in §5.3.1 |

The A → B orientation is conventional (§2.1): the actual flow direction is
returned through the sign of the flow rate (§10.2).

#### 5.5.2 Nodes

| Interface field | Column produced | Notes |
|-----------------|------------------|-------|
| **Node ID** | `node_index` | Integer, consistent with the values used in Node A / Node B. Candidates: `node_id`, `id`, `FID`, `fid`, `OBJECTID`. |
| **Elevation** | `altitude_m` | Candidates: `altitude_m`, `z_m`, `elev`, `altitude`, `alt`, `ALTITUDINE`, `quota`. If the field is absent, h = 0 is assumed. |
| **Reference flag** and value | `node_type` = `reference` | Proposed value: `1`. |
| **Hourly node flow** | `flow_sm3_per_s` | Demand profile (§5.6). If not assigned, all nodes receive the fallback value and the network has no demand. |

### 5.6 Hourly demand profile

**Format.** A string containing up to 24 numeric values separated by the
character set in *Flow separator* (default `;`):

```
1250.0;1180.5;1100.2; … ;1310.8
```

**Unit.** Sm³/h. Conversion to model units is

    D = value / 3600     [sm³/s]                                          (5.1)

**Value selection.** The element whose index equals the *Hour of day* is
extracted; if the string contains fewer values than the requested index, the
last available one is used. **A single value is therefore valid for any hour**
and represents a constant demand.

**Sign convention.**

| Value | Meaning | Effect |
|-------|---------|--------|
| > 0 | Withdrawal | D_i > 0 in (2.3) |
| < 0 | Injection into the network | D_i < 0 in (2.3) |
| 0, empty, `NULL` | Transit node | Assignment of the fallback value (§6.2) |

The sign convention allows injection points (biomethane, electrolysers,
interconnections) to be modelled without resorting to additional reference
nodes, which would also impose the pressure.

### 5.7 Per-node composition

If the node layer contains a field named exactly **`gas_comp`**, its content is
taken as the molar composition of that node, replacing the general composition
(§6.4).

The presence of at least one nodal composition activates the staggered coupling
scheme described in §2.6, reported in the log as
`Composizioni multiple rilevate`. In that case the results include the local
composition (`gas_comp`) and the hydrogen fraction (`h2_pct`) node by node
(§9.2).

### 5.8 Structure of the input files produced

Field separator `;`.

**`sf_nodes.csv`**

```
node_index;pressure_pa;flow_MW;flow_sm3_per_s;altitude_m;temperature_k;gas_composition;node_type;latitude;longitude;flow_type
1;7500000.0;;;10.0;288.15;{'methane': 0.86909, …};reference;;;volumetric
2;;;1e-06;50.0;;;;;;volumetric
3;;;3.33333333;120.0;;;;;;volumetric
```

Reference nodes carry pressure (gauge, cf. 2.2), temperature and composition;
demand nodes carry flow rate and elevation.

**`sf_pipelines.csv`**

```
pipeline_index;inlet_index;outlet_index;diameter_m;length_m;friction_method;roughness_m
1;1;2;0.508;12000.0;chen;1.2e-05
```

Inspecting these two files after running the *Generate CSV* command is the most
direct verification of correct mapping: an error present at this stage
propagates unchanged to the results.

## 6. Tab 1 — Physical and numerical parameters

All parameters in this section reside in the collapsible blocks of Tab 1. For
each, the symbol, unit, default value and admissible range are given.

### 6.1 ⚙ Compressors

Block to be enabled for networks equipped with compressor stations.

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Field and Value** | Node-layer field and value identifying compressor nodes. |
| **Pressure OUT** [bar] | Accepts either the name of a node-layer field holding the discharge pressure, or a constant numeric value (for example `70.0`). |

#### 6.1.1 Formulation

The node marked as a compressor is split into a suction node and a discharge
node, connected by the station. The discharge pressure is imposed as an
additional constraint equation of system (2.3); the flow handled is determined
by the solution.

Since a pressure constraint may require the upstream network to deliver flows
far in excess of those of natural operation, the procedure comprises:

1. a **baseline** simulation without the compressor, with relaxed tolerance and
   a limited iteration count, used to establish flow directions and as the
   starting point (*warm start*) of the subsequent simulation;
2. in case of non-convergence, a **continuation on the control parameter**: the
   discharge target is raised in successive increments from the equilibrium
   value without compressor to the nominal set point, each step initialised
   from the previous one.

Both stages are automatic and traced in the log with the `[compressori]`
prefix.

#### 6.1.2 Warning

In the absence of *Pressure OUT* the engine adopts a fallback compression ratio
of **1.1** (§13.3), insufficient under realistic load and typically a cause of
divergence with collapsing pressures. The plugin issues a non-blocking warning
before starting.

#### 6.1.3 Quantities returned

At convergence the isentropic power absorbed by each station is computed:

    P = cp · T₁ · [ r^((n−1)/n) − 1 ] · ṁ / (η · 10⁶)        [MW]         (6.1)

with r = p_out/p_in the effective ratio, n = cp/cv, ṁ the mass flow rate and η
the efficiency. Values are written to `results_compressors.csv` (§10.4).

### 6.3 ⚙ Solver parameters

Theoretical reference: §2.7.

| Parameter | Unit | Default | Range | Description |
|-----------|------|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Max iterations** | — | 50 | 10–2000 | Maximum number of Newton-Raphson iterations. See the warning in 6.3.1. |
| **Tolerance** | Sm³/s | 0.001 | 1×10⁻⁴–10 | Threshold of criterion (2.14): maximum balance imbalance admitted per node. The adjacent box reports the equivalent in Sm³/h (multiplication by 3600): the default corresponds to 3.6 Sm³/h. |
| **URF** | — | 2.0 | 0.1–20 | Under-relaxation factor of (2.13). **It appears in the denominator**: larger values shorten the step and stabilise the iteration; values below 1 amplify the Newton step. The assigned value acts as the lower bound used by the adaptive scheme (§2.7). |
| **Auto-calibrate tol and URF** | — | off | — | Assigns tolerance and URF as a function of the number of nodes according to table §13.1.1 and, if convergence fails, walks the same ladder towards more relaxed combinations. Enabling it disables the two corresponding fields. |
| **Refine precision** | — | off | — | After the first convergence, repeats the solution with decreasing tolerances — first by factors of 10, then by bisection on a logarithmic scale — down to the limit of 1×10⁻⁴, retaining the result corresponding to the smallest tolerance actually achieved. | **Force Multiprocess** | — | on | — | Execution in independent Python processes, required for parallel simulations (§7.3, §7.4) to make effective use of multiple cores. |

#### 6.3.1 Warning on the maximum iteration count

The number of iterations required depends on the distance between the initial
state and the solution, not on the size of the network. In the verification
runs performed on this plugin (§11.7) the default value of 50 iterations proved
**insufficient** on all three networks tested, including two networks of 4 and
10 nodes; the network of §11 converges in 92 iterations.

Setting **Max iterations = 200** as a starting point is therefore recommended.
The increase carries no penalty: the computation terminates as soon as
criterion (2.14) is met.

### 6.4 Gas Composition

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Base gas** | Composition preset: *SNAM — Mazara del Vallo (Algerian gas)* (default), *SNAM — Gela (Libyan gas)*, *Pure methane (100% CH₄)*, *Typical natural gas (94% CH₄)*, *Pure hydrogen (H₂)*, *Custom…*. Molar fractions are given in §13.3. |
| **H₂ addition** | **Molar** fraction of hydrogen added to the base gas, 0–99 %. The base composition is rescaled proportionally: denoting by x the hydrogen fraction and by y_k the base fractions, the resulting mixture has fractions y_k·(1−x) and hydrogen fraction x. |
| **Export composition** | Composition actually written to the input file. It is updated automatically from the two preceding fields and may be edited manually for compositions not covered by the presets; the preset then becomes *Custom*. |

#### 6.4.1 Admissible components

`methane`, `ethane`, `propane`, `isobutane`, `butane`, `isopentane`,
`pentane`, `hexane`, `nitrogen`, `carbon dioxide`, `helium`, `hydrogen`.

The key for carbon dioxide is **`carbon dioxide`**, with a space. Molar
fractions must sum to unity.

#### 6.4.2 Effect of composition on the hydraulic solution

Composition enters (2.4) through SG and Z and (2.9) through the Reynolds
number. At equal standard volumetric flow rate, a mixture of lower specific
gravity — for example one enriched with hydrogen — exhibits lower pressure loss
and higher velocity, but lower energy content per unit volume. Assessment of a
blending scenario therefore requires joint examination of pressure, velocity,
heating value and Wobbe index (§9.4).

## 7. Execution and output files

### 7.1 Commands

The commands are located in the right-hand column of Tab 1.

| Command | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **↺ Reload layers** | Refreshes the project layer lists. |
| **> Generate CSV** | Performs model construction and mapping and writes the input files, without running the solver. In Mode B it is named *Generate CSV from Layer*. |
| **CSV export only** | Option: results are written to file but no layers are created in QGIS. It appreciably reduces execution time on large networks. |
| **▶ Start Simulation** | Generates the input files and runs the solver. |
| **⏹ Stop** | Interrupts the running computation. |
| **Campaign** | Sequential execution of the hourly batch over a series of days (§7.4). |

Without an output folder, a single simulation uses a temporary system folder;
*Generate CSV* and parallel execution require one explicitly.

### 7.2 Preliminary checks

#### 7.2.1 Blocking conditions

- Mode A: pipeline layer not selected.
- Mode B: collapsed pipeline layer or collapsed node layer not selected.
- Both: absence of reference nodes at the end of model construction.

#### 7.2.2 Warning conditions

The following conditions produce a warning with a request for confirmation;
continuation is permitted but in each case the results are compromised:

| Condition | Consequence |
|-----------|-------------|
| Node layer not selected | No boundary conditions: indeterminate system |
| *Reference flag* field not assigned | All nodes treated as reference |
| Empty reference flag value | All snapped nodes treated as reference |
| Compressors enabled without *Pressure OUT* | Fallback ratio 1.1 (§6.1.2) |

### 7.3 Execution

The computation runs in a separate thread and, with *Force Multiprocess*
enabled, in independent processes: the QGIS interface remains usable.

The log reports, in order:

1. working folder and reference pressure applied;
2. the outcome of input file validation, with the composition adopted. At this
   stage the plugin: writes temperature, pressure and composition on the
   reference nodes; replaces null or missing demands with the fallback flow;
   sets missing elevations to zero; imposes `friction_method = chen`;
3. any messages concerning compressors and multiple compositions;
4. the numerical parameters actually adopted;
5. the outcome: `✓ SIMULAZIONE CONVERSA in N iterazioni!` or a non-convergence
   notice followed by `Estraggo l'ultima soluzione calcolata…`;
6. the path to the results and the elapsed time.

At the end of a single simulation the result layers are created and the
interface switches automatically to Tab 2.

#### 7.3.1 Hourly batch

The **Simulate 24 hours in parallel** option runs the 24 hourly scenarios
simultaneously. The degree of parallelism is set from the estimated number of
physical cores and is stated in the log. The final summary reports the number
of converged hours, the list of those that did not converge and the total time.

### 7.4 Multi-day campaign

The **Campaign** command repeats the hourly batch over a series of days. The
network (pipeline layer) is common; what varies are the node layers,
representing the demand profiles of individual days.

The procedure requires: the source of the days (files on disk in `.geojson`,
`.shp` or equivalent format, or layers already loaded in the project); the
output folder; and confirmation, with the total number of simulations
(days × 24).

During a campaign the *CSV export only* option is forced and dialogs are
suppressed: a day failing the checks of §7.2 is skipped and recorded in the
log, without interrupting the run. The final summary reports days completed,
days with 24 converged hours out of 24, total converged simulations and overall
time.

### 7.5 Outcome of the simulation

The solver returns a result even when convergence fails (§2.7.1). That result
is the last computed iterate: **it does not satisfy the nodal balances and does
not represent a physically admissible state**, despite having the appearance of
an ordinary solution.

The outcome is declared in three ways: the log message, the colour of the
closing line and — the decisive element — the **suffix of the results folder
name**, `_OK` or `_NONCONV`. Checking that suffix must precede any use of the
values produced.

### 7.6 Organisation of the output files

Each run generates a progressive subfolder; no file is ever overwritten.

```
<output folder>/
├─ _warmstart_seed.csv              pressures of the last converged solution
├─ run_001/
│  ├─ input/
│  │  ├─ sf_nodes.csv               generated input data (§5.8)
│  │  ├─ sf_pipelines.csv
│  │  └─ sf_compressors.csv         if compressor stations are present
│  ├─ output/
│  │  └─ convergence_log.txt        per-iteration diagnostics (§12.10)
│  └─ h00_OK/                       results for hour 0, converged
│     ├─ results_nodes.csv
│     ├─ results_pipes.csv
│     ├─ results_compressors.csv
│     ├─ run_info.json           provenance of the run (§7.8)
│     ├─ R-N-<name>_#1.gpkg
│     └─ R-P-<name>_#1.gpkg
└─ run_002/ …
```

Notes:

- result files use `;` as separator;
- re-running the same hour with a different outcome removes the folder
  corresponding to the previous outcome, preventing contradictory results from
  coexisting;
- result layers are persisted as **GeoPackage** in the same folder: the layer
  present in QGIS refers to that file and survives closing the project;
- the file `_warmstart_seed.csv`, written at the end of each converged single
  simulation, is reused as the initial state by subsequent runs (§2.7),
  reducing their iteration count.

### 7.7 Layers produced in the project

| Designation | Content |
|-------------|---------|
| `Condotte #N` / `Nodi #N` | Collapsed network, actually simulated (Mode A) |
| `R-N-<name> #N` | Nodal results (§9) |
| `R-P-<name> #N` | Pipeline results (§10) |
| `R-P-orig-<name> #N` | Results carried back onto the original geometry, if requested (§6.2) |

The progressive index `#N` increases at every run: **layers from previous
simulations are not removed**. The choice enables direct comparison between
scenarios (§8.4) but leads to a progressive accumulation of legend entries.

### 7.8 Traceability of results

The plugin version and the calculation engine version are **independent**: the
plugin uses a fork of GasNetSim containing modifications absent from any
released upstream version, so the engine version string alone is not sufficient
to identify the code executed.

Every run therefore records both identities in two places:

1. the **first line of the log**, in the form
   `GasNetSim_4Qgis 0.9.0 · motore GasNetSim 0.2.2 (improvement/modular-simulation-architecture @ 6079ec1)`;
2. the **`run_info.json`** file, written in the results folder alongside the
   CSVs, containing:

| Block | Content |
|-------|---------|
| `plugin` | plugin name and version |
| `engine` | declared engine version, branch and commit of the clone it is imported from, installation path |
| `run` | date and time, simulated hour, convergence outcome, iteration count, computation time |
| `parameters` | reference pressure, temperature, roughness, fallback flows, maximum iteration count, tolerance, URF, auto-calibration, refinement, gas composition |

The file is written as separate JSON, rather than as a CSV header, so that the
result files remain readable by any parser without additional options.

The content of `run_info.json` is the minimum information to be reported when
documenting a published result: it identifies the code and the parameters with
which it was produced, and allows the computation to be repeated even after the
files have been moved or reprocessed. Note that the engine commit is read from
the clone the package is imported from: an installation performed by cloning a
*branch* rather than a tag or a specific commit does not guarantee that others
will obtain the same engine.

#### 8.1.1 Rendering criteria

Classification is **graduated with quantile classes**, with approximately
uniform population per class. For pipelines the line width is made proportional
to the absolute flow rate, so as to highlight the transport hierarchy. The
`h2_pct` field uses a dedicated scale, progressing from white/yellow (zero
fraction) to red.

The use of quantile classes implies that the colour scale is **relative to the
individual layer**: two simulations rendered with the same settings are not
comparable by colour, only by value. For quantitative comparison, use the
numerical values or a manual classification with fixed intervals, available
through the standard QGIS tools.

### 8.2 Settings for the next simulation

Defines the fields that the next simulation will produce and their initial
rendering. It has no effect on results already computed.

**Nodes.** Initial colouring field (default *Pressure*), sizing field (default
none) and selection of the fields to be produced. Enabled by default: pressure,
volumetric consumption, energy consumption, temperature, specific gravity, Z
factor, heating value, Wobbe index, H₂ fraction, composition and throughput;
disabled: density, standard density, molar mass and node type.

**Pipelines.** Initial colouring field (default *Flow*) and field selection:
enabled by default signed flow, absolute flow, velocity, average pressure and
specific gravity; disabled Z factor and density.

**Show flow direction arrows**: disabled by default.

A field not selected here is not written to the layer and is therefore
unavailable for the rendering of §8.1 until a new simulation is run. The nodal
fields `volumetric_flow_sm3s`, `energy_flow_mw` and `node_type` are present in
the QGIS layers only and not in the CSV files (§9.1).

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Layer** | Layer to be exported. |
| **Decimals** | Decimal digits of numeric values. |
| **Export CSV** | Selection of the destination path. |

The function complements the `results_nodes.csv` and `results_pipes.csv` files
produced automatically (§7.6): it allows export of a subset, of a different

### 8.4 Comparison between scenarios

The accumulation of result layers (§7.7) allows direct comparison:

2. modify the parameter under analysis — for example the hydrogen fraction

3. select alternately layers `#1` and `#2` on the same field, in block §8.1.

non-comparability of quantile colour scales.

### 9.2 Composition-dependent quantities

With a single composition, `sp_gravity`, `hhv_mj_sm3`, `wobbe_mj_sm3`,
`molar_mass`, `std_density_kg_sm3` and `h2_pct` are uniform across the network,
whereas `z_factor` and `density_kg_m3` vary with the local pressure.

Where injections of differing composition are present (§5.7), all
composition-dependent quantities vary from node to node according to the mixing
field solved with the scheme of §2.6. The distribution of `h2_pct` then
describes the dilution of hydrogen away from the injection points and
constitutes the quantity of interest in partial blending studies.

### 9.3 Expected orders of magnitude

Values computed for the default gas (SNAM Mazara del Vallo) and for the same
mixture with 13.92 % molar hydrogen, at 76 bar and 288.15 K.

| Quantity | Mazara | Mazara + 13.92 % H₂ | Change |
|----------|--------|----------------------|--------|
| SG | 0.6323 | 0.5539 | −12.4 % |
| M | 18.32 g/mol | 16.05 g/mol | −12.4 % |
| Z | 0.818 | 0.879 | +7.5 % |
| ρ (76 bar) | ≈ 71 kg/m³ | ≈ 58 kg/m³ | −18 % |
| ρ_b | 0.8172 kg/sm³ | 0.7160 kg/sm³ | −12.4 % |
| HHV | 42.12 MJ/sm³ | 38.04 MJ/sm³ | −9.7 % |
| W | 52.98 MJ/sm³ | 51.11 MJ/sm³ | −3.5 % |

The values were obtained with the plugin's calculation engine under the stated

### 9.4 Reading criteria

**Minimum pressure.** Adequacy assessment is carried out on the ascending
ordering of `pressure_bar`, or on the thematic rendering of the same field: the
lowest-pressure nodes identify the critical sections of the network.

**Anomalous uniformity.** A nearly uniform pressure field, together with
`throughput_sm3h` values close to zero, indicates that demands have not been
acquired (§12.7), not that the network is oversized.

**Gas quality.** HHV and Wobbe index must be examined jointly: increasing the
hydrogen fraction reduces both, but to a different extent (§9.3), and the
governing regulatory constraint for Italian networks concerns specific gravity
(§13.4). The reduction of the Wobbe index, modest compared with that of the
heating value, is the quantity governing compatibility with end-user
appliances.

**Consistency with the boundary condition.** The pressure at the reference node
must equal the assigned value increased by 1.013 bar (2.2). A discrepancy
indicates that the reference node is not the expected one, or that more than
one exists.

## 10. Pipeline and compressor quantities

### 10.1 Pipeline quantities

| Field | Symbol | Unit | Definition |
|-------|--------|------|------------|
| `flow_sm3h` | Q | Sm³/h | Volumetric flow rate at standard conditions, **signed** (§10.2). Obtained from (2.4). |
| `flow_abs_sm3h` | \|Q\| | Sm³/h | Magnitude of the flow rate. Default field for colouring and for modulating line width. |

### 10.2 Sign convention and flow direction

The A → B orientation is assigned during model construction (§2.1) and presumes
no knowledge of how the network operates. The actual direction follows from the
solution:

    Q > 0  →  flow from A to B
    Q < 0  →  flow from B to A

A change in the sign of a pipeline between two scenarios is a result of
interest: it identifies stretches supplied from different directions as
operating conditions change, information relevant when assessing new injection
points.

The arrow rendering (§8.1) follows the sign of `flow_sm3h`.

### 10.3 Velocity

Velocity cannot be obtained by dividing the standard flow rate by the
cross-sectional area: the flow rate refers to conditions (2.1), whereas the gas
travels at operating conditions. The conversion requires the local density, a
function of p, T and composition through the equation of state (§2.5).

At transmission pressures, values of the order of 0.3–10 m/s correspond to flow
rates of tens of thousands of Sm³/h. Comparison with erosion limits is covered
in §13.5; it is recalled that the allowable velocity **decreases** as pressure
increases, the gas being denser.

Velocities of the order of 10⁻³ m/s across the whole network normally indicate
that demands have not been acquired (§12.7).

### 10.4 Compressor station quantities

Contained in `results_compressors.csv`, produced when the network includes
compressor stations (§6.1).

| Field | Unit | Definition |
|-------|------|------------|
| `compressor_index` | — | Station identifier. |
| `inlet_node` / `outlet_node` | — | Suction and discharge nodes; the latter is generated by splitting the marked node (§6.1.1). |
| `inlet_pressure_bar` / `outlet_pressure_bar` | bar (absolute) | Pressures at convergence. |
| `compression_ratio` | — | Effective ratio r = p_out / p_in. A value close to unity indicates that the station is not compressing. |
| `flow_sm3h` | Sm³/h | Flow handled. |
| `mass_flow_kg_s` | kg/s | Mass flow rate, ṁ = ρ_b · Q. |
| `power_MW` | MW | Isentropic power absorbed, eq. (6.1). Zero for r ≤ 1. |
| `energy_MWh` | MWh | Energy corresponding to the simulated hour. |

Absorbed power is computed **only when convergence has been reached**: in a
`_NONCONV` folder the compressor values are meaningless.

### 10.5 Consistency checks

**Nodal balance.** At a branching node the flow of the upstream pipeline must
equal the sum of the downstream flows, net of the nodal demand and of the
residual imbalance permitted by tolerance (2.14). The expected discrepancy is
of the order of tol × 3600 Sm³/h: with the default value it amounts to
3.6 Sm³/h, entirely negligible against flows of tens of thousands of Sm³/h but
not zero. §11.6 provides a quantitative example of this check.

**Continuity of properties.** In the absence of multiple injections,
`sp_gravity` and the other composition-dependent quantities must be constant
across all pipelines. Variations indicate the presence of unintended nodal
compositions (§5.7).

**Consistency with pressure losses.** At equal flow rate, larger-diameter
pipelines must exhibit appreciably smaller pressure drops, since C_p ∝ D^2.5 in
(2.5). Contrary behaviour normally indicates errors in the diameter unit
(§12.8).

## 12. Convergence diagnostics

Non-convergence occurs when criterion (2.14) is not satisfied within the
prescribed number of iterations. The solver nonetheless returns the last
iterate, marked by the `_NONCONV` suffix of the results folder (§7.5): an
intermediate state, devoid of physical meaning, which does not satisfy the
nodal balances.

It is emphasised that convergence is a property of the problem — topology,
demands, diameters, initial state — in combination with the algorithm employed,
and not of the program. Only the default values of the numerical parameters and
the coupling scheme adopted (§12.3) depend on the latter; all the remaining
causes listed below reside in the data or in the network.

This section lists the causes in order of observed frequency, with the
corresponding checks.

### 12.1 Insufficient iteration count

**Frequency: the highest.** The number of iterations required depends on the
distance between the initial state and the solution, not on the size of the
network. In the tests carried out (§11.7) the default value of 50 iterations
proved insufficient on all three networks examined:

| Network | Nodes | Outcome with 50 iterations | Outcome with 200 |
|---------|-------|-----------------------------|-------------------|
| Verification case (§11) | 4 | does not converge | converges in 92 iterations |
| `toy_network_h2` | 10 | does not converge | not determined |
| `Sicily_transmission_network` | 340 | does not converge (residual 1×10⁻¹ Sm³/s) | not determined |

**Action.** Set *Max iterations* to 200, and to 500 if necessary. The increase
carries no penalty: execution terminates as soon as (2.14) is satisfied.

### 12.2 Insufficiently damped Newton step

If the residual oscillates without decreasing — a frequent condition where
pipelines of very different diameter are directly connected — the step length
is excessive.

**Action.** **Increase** the URF, for example from 2.0 to 4.0. Recall that the
URF appears in the denominator of (2.13): larger values shorten the step and
stabilise the iteration; values below 1 amplify the Newton step and should be
reserved for networks that converge readily and for which the iteration count
is to be reduced.

The tolerance may subsequently be relaxed, from 0.001 to 0.01 Sm³/s (36 Sm³/h
of admitted imbalance per node, negligible on networks carrying hundreds of
thousands of Sm³/h).

The *Auto-calibrate tol and URF* option automates this search, walking the
ladder of §13.1.1 towards progressively more relaxed combinations. Check in the
log which values actually achieved convergence, since the tolerances employed
are appreciably wider than the default.

### 12.3 Coupling scheme adopted

Convergence is not a property of the software: it depends on the problem —
topology, demands, diameters, initial state — in combination with the algorithm
used to solve it. The plugin does, however, determine three elements of the
algorithm, and these are the only ones on which it acts:

1. the maximum iteration count (§12.1), whose default value is 50;
2. the coupling scheme between the hydraulic problem and composition tracking;
3. the tolerances of the outer loop of the staggered scheme, set to
   `coupling_tol = tol × 10⁻²` and `coupling_max_iter = max_iter` in place of
   the engine defaults (10⁻⁴ and 20).

#### 12.3.1 Selection criterion

The staggered scheme is enabled when at least one non-reference node carries its
own composition in the `sf_nodes.csv` file (§5.7); otherwise the direct scheme
is adopted. The criterion is syntactic in nature — the presence of a value in a
field — and not numerical: it in no way depends on the difficulty of the
problem.

#### 12.3.2 Why the scheme matters

In the direct scheme the mixture assigned to each pipeline is redefined from the
sign of the flow rate at every residual evaluation. If nodal compositions differ
from one another, a pipeline that reverses its flow during the iteration changes
its mixture and with it the gas properties: the residual function becomes
discontinuous in x and the line search stalls without achieving a reduction of
the residual (cf. the messages of §12.10). The staggered scheme removes the
discontinuity by freezing the composition during the Newton step and updating it
in a separate step, at fixed pressures, with relaxation.

It follows that on a network with a single composition the two schemes are
substantially equivalent, since redefining the mixture always returns the same
mixture. In that case non-convergence is to be attributed to §12.1 and §12.2,
not to the scheme.

#### 12.3.3 Quality of the solution

The inner step of the staggered scheme is the same Newton solver as the direct
scheme, invoked with the same tolerance and the same maximum iteration count;
the inner iteration is considered complete only when criterion (2.14) is
satisfied. Convergence of the staggered scheme is therefore not obtained with a
weaker criterion: two conditions on the outer loop are added to criterion
(2.14) — maximum pressure variation between two outer iterations not exceeding
max(10; tol × 10⁵) Pa, and composition variation not exceeding `coupling_tol`.

#### 12.3.4 Action

On networks with differing compositions, should stalling of the line search be
observed, check the log for the line `Composizioni multiple rilevate`: its
absence indicates that the composition field was not read and that the problem
is being solved with the direct scheme. The field is named `gas_comp` in the
node layer (§5.7); assigning a composition to a transit node does not alter the
physics of the problem when that composition is identical to the reference
node's, but it switches the solution scheme.

### 12.4 Absence of reference nodes

The simulation is stopped before starting (§7.2.1). No record of the node layer
satisfies the field = value condition set in *Reference field*.

**Checks.** Existence of the field; presence of the expected value (consider
the forms `1`, `1.0`, `"1"`); in Mode A, whether the node lies within the snap
radius (§6.2): a correct node further from the pipeline than the radius set is
not recognised.

### 12.5 All nodes treated as reference

The symmetric condition to the preceding one, and more insidious because it
**does not prevent convergence**: with the flag value left empty, all snapped
nodes assume imposed pressure. System (2.3) is left without unknowns,
convergence is immediate and the results are void of content.

**Symptoms.** Identical or nearly identical pressures at all nodes; flow rates
inconsistent with demands; a very low iteration count.

### 12.6 Disconnected components

A component without a reference node renders the system indeterminate. On
vector data of non-modelling origin it is the most frequent cause after that of
§12.1.

**Action.** Procedure of §4.5. Note that removal of residual components is
necessary even where they correspond to genuinely isolated portions of the
network.

### 12.7 Demands not acquired

**Symptoms.** Nearly uniform pressures; velocities of the order of 10⁻³ m/s;
`throughput_sm3h` close to zero throughout the network.

**Checks, in order.**

1. Assignment of the *Hourly node flow* field. In Mode A it resides in the
   *⚙ Advanced options* block (§6.2) and not in the main block.
2. The value reported by the preview beneath *Hour of day* (§5.2).
3. Correspondence between the separator set (§6.2) and the one actually present
   in the data: a mismatch causes a single uninterpretable value to be read and
   the fallback value to be applied.
4. Units of the values: (5.1) presumes Sm³/h.

### 12.8 Inconsistent pressures or anomalous velocities

**Predominant cause: diameter unit.** The default of the *Unit* menu is
`inches`. A field expressed in metres and interpreted as inches reduces
diameters by a factor of 39.37; through the D^2.5 dependence in (2.5) the
transport capacity is reduced by more than nine orders of magnitude, causing
pressures to collapse. The converse error produces a network that carries any
flow rate without appreciable losses.

**Secondary cause: elevations.** In Mode A elevations do not come from the node
layer (§5.4). The absence of a DEM with two-dimensional geometries nullifies
term (2.8); erroneous elevations, or elevations in units other than metres,
make it dominant.

### 12.9 Compressor stations

Omitting *Pressure OUT* entails adoption of the fallback ratio of 1.1
(§6.1.2), with near-certain divergence under load.

Where the configuration is correct but the network fails to converge, examine
the `[compressori]` lines of the log: the procedure first runs a simulation
without the compressor. Failure of the latter to converge indicates that the
problem lies with the network and not with the station.

### 12.10 Per-iteration diagnostics

For single simulations the file
`<folder>/run_NNN/output/convergence_log.txt` records the detail of each
iteration. The significant lines are those concerning the **equations with the
largest residual**, which identify the node whose balance fails to close and
the current pressure at that node.

Persistence of the same node among the critical equations indicates a local
cause — demand incompatible with the capacity of the connected pipeline, an
isolated node, an incorrect diameter — whose removal is preferable to any
modification of the numerical parameters.

Messages such as `trust region active`, `line search` and `stalled at
iteration` indicate that the step was repeatedly shortened without obtaining a
reduction of the residual: a condition typical of a discontinuity in the
residual function, attributable to flow reversals or to switching in the
compressor logic.

### 12.11 Long computation times

- Enable *CSV export only* when the layers are not required: building the
  symbology on large networks has an appreciable cost.
- Keep *Force Multiprocess* enabled for parallel runs.
- Avoid output folders subject to cloud synchronisation: writing the
  intermediate files is slowed accordingly.
- In an exploratory phase, reduce the iteration count and relax the tolerance;
  refinement (§6.3) is a concluding operation, not a preliminary one.

### 12.12 Checklist before using the results

1. Suffix of the results folder: `_OK`.
2. Presence in the log of `✓ SIMULAZIONE CONVERSA` and the final tolerance
   value.
3. Nodal balance verified within tolerance (§10.5, §11.6).
4. Pressure at the reference node equal to the assigned value plus 1.013 bar.
5. Velocities and pressures within plausible ranges for the pipeline class
   (§13.5).

### 14.2 Glossary

**Best iterate** — The result returned when the simulation does not converge:
the last computed state, which does not satisfy the nodal balances.

**Blending** — Injection of hydrogen, or another gas, into a network designed
for natural gas.

**Chen correlation** — Explicit expression (2.9) for the friction factor,
applied to every pipeline.

**Compressor (compressor station)** — Component that raises pressure; in the
plugin it is configured by imposing the discharge pressure, and the results
include the power and energy absorbed.

**Disconnected component (island)** — Subgraph not connected to the main graph.
Without a reference node it renders the system indeterminate.

**Erosional velocity** — Velocity threshold beyond which high-velocity flow
causes erosion of the internal wall, eq. (13.1).

**GERG-2008** — Reference equation of state for natural gas, standardised in
ISO 20765-2.

**General flow equation** — Relation (2.4) between flow rate and the difference
of the squares of the pressures, inclusive of the elevation term and of
real-gas properties.

**Junction (transit) node** — Node without a demand of its own, where pipelines
connect. It receives the fallback demand (§6.2).

**Line search** — Progressive shortening of the Newton step until a reduction
of the residual is obtained.

**MOP** — Maximum operating pressure; basis of the classification by class
(§13.4.1).

**Nodal balance** — Mass conservation equation written at a node, eq. (2.3). It
constitutes the governing equation of the model.

**Non-converged case (NONCONV)** — Outcome in which criterion (2.14) is not
satisfied within the maximum iteration count. The result returned is the last
iterate and does not represent a physically admissible state.

**Placeholder flow** — Demand of negligible magnitude assigned to transit nodes
to avoid identically zero rows in the Jacobian matrix.

**Reference node** — Fixed-pressure node; boundary condition of the problem. It
is also the node at which the composition of the injected gas is assigned.

**Residual** — Value taken by F(x) for a given state: it measures the imbalance
of the nodal balances. The stopping criterion is formulated on its maximum.

**Snap** — Geometric unification of mutually close endpoints (§4.3).

**Snap radius** — Maximum distance within which a node of the node layer is
associated with a pipeline endpoint during the collapse (§6.2).

**Specific gravity** — Ratio between the molar mass of the mixture and that of
air, eq. (2.10). A quantity subject to regulatory constraint in Italian
networks (§13.4.2).

**Standard conditions** — Reference state for volume measurement: 288.15 K and
101 325 Pa, eq. (2.1).

**Steady state** — Operating condition at constant demand, to which the entire
formulation refers. Transients are not modelled.

**Direct / staggered scheme** — Coupling modes between the hydraulic problem
and composition tracking (§2.6). The staggered scheme is enabled where multiple
nodal compositions are present.

**Throughput (nodal)** — Total flow entering a node from the connected
pipelines; distinct from the nodal demand.

**Topological collapse** — Construction of the computational graph from the
vector geometry: explosion of polylines, identification of coincident
endpoints, generation of nodes and edges, computation of lengths (§5.3.1).

**Under-relaxation (URF)** — Divisor of the Newton increment, eq. (2.13).
Larger values damp the iteration.

**Warm start (seed)** — Initialisation of the computation with the pressures of
a previously converged solution, in order to reduce the iteration count (§7.6).

**Wobbe index** — Ratio of the higher heating value to the square root of
specific gravity, eq. (2.11). Index of interchangeability between fuel gases.

**Z factor** — See *Compressibility factor*: departure of real gas behaviour
from ideal, computed with GERG-2008.
